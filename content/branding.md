I've been discussing branding and marketing recently and have come across a a laundry list of patterns which put me off certain products. I think [Notion](https://www.notion.com/) is a good example of a strong brand which is going off the rails.

Looking at the homepage you land on a massive screen serious which tells you to "think together". This is a bit of a diversion from their fun little mascot and generally rounded aesthetic. They are now "serious".

![notion](/images/notion.png)

I think Notion achieves "think together". These sort of slogans come out of marketing teams which are trying to take a step back and find something that encapsules the scope of what their product achieves. The problem is when you take 10 steps back and it becomes meaningless. You can argue that "think together" is what most products are trying to do. I still know nothing about your product. What is it that you do? Moving on to the their centre element.

Ok sure they want you to watch a video. I think that this is the another mistake. I should be able to statically see the value that you're providing with little frill. Give me information! There's something in your brain that instantly switches on when you're viewing marketing material. This switch is a nessesary adaption in the modern world because most marketing people are full of it. Your brain is looking for signal in a sea of noise. Let's say that I'm willing to watch your video (I probably don't even want to do this as a new user but you've put it front and centre so it's really important right?):


![notion-space](/images/notion-space.png)


The video starts and you're drawing parallels (as a text editing program!) to the Apollo 11 moon landing? One of mankinds greatest achievements. My bullshit meter is off the charts at this point. You cannot seriously assert or associate yourself as a text editing program anywhere near this sort of achievement without coming across as a conman.  Would you talk to a person which made these sorts of assertions? "Hey my program is on tier with the achivement of landing on the moon. I'm groundbreaking". You would probably think of them as insane and ask for proof. You probably wouldn't give them money. The closest thing I can think of is grandstanding. In a product I think you're allowed to be bold and say cool stuff but it has to be at least grounded in some sort of reality.


In summary just say what you do up front in a static manner. This strategy might be more effective than trying to grandstand. That's getting old and new generations are not buying into it. Your slick website and animations are not going to convince them otherwise. There is a case that good marketing material doesn't make it feel like you're being sold to and actually provides value in exchange for the words that you're reading. I think this can be a standout point in a time where words are increasingly being detached from their meaning through LLMs and corprate nullspeak.
